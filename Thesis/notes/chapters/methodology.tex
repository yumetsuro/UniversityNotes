\section{Modularity in RNNs}
\label{sec:method:modularity}
In dynamical systems theory, a system is said to be \emph{modular} if it can be decomposed into distinct 
subsystems (modules) each of which has its own internal dynamics while also interactting with other modules.
Such composition allows for a complex global behaviour to emerge from the interactions of simpler components.
At the same time, analyzing the system in terms of local module dynamics can provide control on information flow and interpretability benefits.

Modularity has been extensively studied in neuroscience, where large-scale brain networks exhibit modular organization with specialized regions that acts as hubs for information integration.
Empirical studies from \cite{bullmore2009complex} have shown that neural systems are composed of densely connected subnetworks that interact globally as a sparse system \todo{che sono connesse in modo sparso forse è meglio?}, supporting
both integration of information and specialized processing 

More generally, studies in complex systems and network dynamics have shown that modular organization 
can enhance robustness by inducing a separation of time scales, such that dynamical processes remain
 confined within subsystems for extended periods before spreading globally. 
 This structure allows perturbations to decay locally and supports stable large-scale 
 dynamics under appropriate stability conditions \cite{Lambiotte_Schaub_2022}.

\section{Deep Modular Reservoirs}
\label{sec:method:deep_modular}
We model a deep reservoir as a composition of $L$ layers or modules. 
Each module $l\in\{1,\dots,L\}$ has state $\mathbf{h}^{(l)}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_l}$ and internal recurrent connectivity $\mathbf{W}^{(l)}$.
The key methodological contribution is to compare two architectures of deep modular reservoirs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Deep Cycle Reservoir} where we emply \emph{cycle/ring} recurrent topology, inspired by Simple Cycle Reservoirs (SCR), to provide strong memory traces with minimal parameters.
    \item \textbf{Antisymmetric deep reservoir} with \emph{forward and backward} coupling between adjacent modules, designed to promote stable dynamics and improve gradient-free memory propagation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Global state and dynamics}
Let $\mathbf{H}(t)$ be the global reservoir state at time $t$ obtained by feeding the input $\mathbf{u}(t)$ into the first module and propagating through the layers.
By stacking the states of all modules, we have the final feature vector used for the readout is made
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H}(t) = \bigl[
    \mathbf{h}^{(1)}(t),
    \mathbf{h}^{(2)}(t),
    \mathbf{h}^{(L)}(t)
    \bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^{N}; \quad N = \sum_{l=1}^L n_l.
    \label{eq:method:feature_concat}
\end{equation}
Where $N$ is the total reservoir size and $n_l$ is the size of module $l$.
The global dynamics of the reservoir can be written in the compact form:
obtained after the application of the activation function and leaky integration.

We define the pre-activation vector
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:method:pre_activation}
\mathbf z(t+1) =
\mathbf W_{\text{tot}} \mathbf H(t)
+ \mathbf W_{\text{in}} \mathbf u(t+1)\footnote{\text{This input term is just for the input not the inter-layer connection}}
+ \mathbf b .
\end{equation}

The reservoir state is then updated as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:method:state_update_general}
\mathbf H(t+1) =
(1-\alpha)\mathbf H(t) + \alpha \tanh(\mathbf z(t+1)).
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{H}(t+1) = \tanh\left(\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}} \, \mathbf{H}(t)\right),
\end{equation}
where $\tanh(\cdot)$ acts element-wise on the state vector and
$\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ encodes both intra-module and inter-module connections.
 $\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is a block matrix that encodes both intra-module and inter-module connections.
For a simple Leaky-ESN with 1 layer, the $\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}$ is simply the recurrent weight matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{rec}}$ of the reservoir.

Finally, the structure of $\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}$ depends on the chosen architecture, a randomly connected module architecture would have 
a block diagonal structure, plus random connections between modules.

\section{Deep Cycle Reservoir}
\label{sec:method:cycle}
We introduce \textbf{Deep Cycle Reservoir} (DCR) as class of deep reservoir, inspired by methods discussed in sec \ref{sec:method:cycle}.
We tried to apply modularity concepts to a delayed version of SCR.

\subsection{Motivation or Discussion?}
In a conventional deepESN, information flows unidirectionally 
from the input through the stacked reservoirs to the readout.
But it lacks a feedback path that might allow higher‑level temporal abstractions
to influence lower levels. A cyclic deep reservoir introduces a feedback connection 
from the output of the last reservoir layer back to the first layer.
The motivation for closing the loop is to allow information processed at deeper layers 
(capturing longer‑term or low‑frequency features) to inform the processing of new inputs.
This can enrich the reservoir dynamics and, in principle, improve tasks requiring both short and long‑term memory.

However, a naïve instantaneous cycle where the output of the deepest layer at time step $t$
is fed directly into the first layer at the same time step creates an algebraic loop. 
In the discrete‑time update equation of an ESN, the next state depends on the previous 
state and the current input; if the current state of a high‑level layer 
instantly modifies the state of the first layer, the system simultaneously depends 
on its own output. Such instantaneous feedback can destabilize the dynamics, (how...?).

Moreover, note that when each deepESN layer is
fed the input directly (as in deepESN‑IA \cite{GALLICCHIO201787}), the intrinsic time‑scale separation is attenuated.

%\subsection{Architecture of Deep Cycle Reservoir}
\label{sec:method:cycle_architecture}
We can define the equation for the DCR as follows, omitting biases for simplicity:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:method:cycle_dynamics}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{h}^{(1)}(t) &= (1-\alpha)\,\mathbf{h}^{(1)}(t-1)
+ \alpha\,\tanh\Big(
\mathbf{W}_{\text{in}}^{(1)} \mathbf{u}(t)
+ \mathbf{W}_{\text{rec}}^{(1)} \mathbf{h}^{(1)}(t-1)
+ \mathbf{W}_{\text{cyc}}^{(L)} \mathbf{h}^{(L)}(t-1)}
\Big), \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{h}^{(l)}(t) &= (1-\alpha)\,\mathbf{h}^{(l)}(t-1)
+ \alpha\,\tanh\Big(
\mathbf{W}_{\text{in}}^{(l)} \mathbf{u}(t)
+ \mathbf{W}_{\text{rec}}^{(l)} \mathbf{h}^{(l)}(t-1)
+ \mathbf{W}_{\text{cyc}}^{(l-1)} \mathbf{h}^{(l-1)}(t-1)}
\Big).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We have a global matrix $W_{DCR}$ with:

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:method:dcr}
    W_{DCR}=\WtotCycle
\end{equation}

Where each $\Wrec{l}$ is scaled by $\rho$ and $\Wcyc{l}$ is initialized as same as recurrent matrices but can have different $\rho$ scaling factor.
The $\rho$ controls the effective spectral radius. \footnote{For a pure permutation cycle, the eigenvalues lie on the unit circle, and scaling by $\rho$ sets their magnitude.}

\subsubsection{Echo State Property of Deep Cycle Reservoir}
\label{sec:method:esp_def}
To formally characterize the stability of the DCR, we adopt the definition of the ESP as formulated for deep architectures in \cite{gallicchio2017echo}. 
We introduce the notation $\hat{F}$ to denote the iterated version of the DCR global state transition function defined in Eq. \ref{eq:method:state_update_general}.

Given an input sequence of arbitrary finite length $N_s$, denoted as $\mathbf{s} \in (\mathbb{R}^{N_U})^*$, and a global initial state $\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$, the function 
$\hat{F}(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{H})$ represents the global state of the network after being driven 
by the sequence $\mathbf{s}$ starting from $\mathbf{H}$.

The iterated map is defined recursively:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:method:iterated_map}
\hat{F} : (\mathbb{R}^{N_U})^* \times \mathbb{R}^{N} \to \mathbb{R}^{N}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\hat{F}(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{H}) = 
\begin{cases} 
\mathbf{H} & \text{if } \mathbf{s} = [] \\
F(\mathbf{u}(k), \hat{F}([\mathbf{u}(1), \dots, \mathbf{u}(k-1)], \mathbf{H})) & \text{if } \mathbf{s} = [\mathbf{u}(1), \dots, \mathbf{u}(k)]
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $[]$ denotes the null sequence.

Accordingly, the ESP for the Deep Cycle Reservoir is defined as follows:

\begin{definition}[Echo State Property of DCR]
\label{def:esp_dcr}
Assuming a Deep Cycle Reservoir whose global dynamics are governed by the function $F$. 
The network has \emph{echo states} if for any input sequence of length $k$, $\mathbf{s}_k = [\mathbf{u}(1), \dots, \mathbf{u}(k)]$, 
and for all pairs of initial global states $\mathbf{H}, \mathbf{H}' \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$, then:
\begin{equation}
||\hat{F}(\mathbf{s}_k, \mathbf{H}) - \hat{F}(\mathbf{s}_k, \mathbf{H}')|| \to 0 \quad \text{as} \quad k \to \infty.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

From a dynamical systems perspective, the DCR implements an input-driven discrete-time non-linear dynamical system $F$,
 mapping the external input $\mathbf{u}(t)$ and the global state $\mathbf{H}(t-1)$ to the next state $\mathbf{H}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H}(t) = F(\mathbf{u}(t), \mathbf{H}(t-1)).
\end{equation}
As defined in Eq. \ref{eq:method:cycle_dynamics}, the global state transition is 
governed by the global weight matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{cycle}}$ defined in \ref{sec:method:}.

\subsubsection{Local Linearization of DCR Dynamics (Jacobian)}
To investigate the stability of the DCR, we analyze the local first-order approximation of the system.
The linearized dynamics of the DCR around a state $\mathbf{H}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$ (assuming zero input for stability analysis)
are governed by the Jacobian matrix of the state transition function $\mathbf{J}(t) = \frac{\partial \mathbf{H}(t)}{\partial \mathbf{H}(t-1)}$.

Considering the block structure of the DCR, where connections exist not only between subsequent layers
 (feedforward) but also from the last layer to the first (feedback), the Jacobian $\mathbf{J}(t)$ 
 takes the form of a \textbf{block-cyclic matrix}. Linearizing around the zero state ($\mathbf{H}_0 = \mathbf{0}$) where $\tanh'(0)=1$, the Jacobian simplifies to (for a detailed derivation see Appendix \ref{ch:appendix-A}):

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{0}) =
\begin{pmatrix}
(1-\alpha)\mathbf{I} + \alpha\mathbf{W}_{\text{rec}}^{(1)} & \mathbf{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \alpha\mathbf{W}_{\text{cyc}}^{(1)} \\
\alpha\mathbf{W}_{\text{cyc}}^{(2)} & (1-\alpha)\mathbf{I} + \alpha\mathbf{W}_{\text{rec}}^{(2)} & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{0} & \alpha\mathbf{W}_{\text{cyc}}^{(3)} & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & (1-\alpha)\mathbf{I} + \alpha\mathbf{W}_{\text{rec}}^{(L-1)} & \mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \alpha\mathbf{W}_{\text{cyc}}^{(L)} & (1-\alpha)\mathbf{I} + \alpha\mathbf{W}_{\text{rec}}^{(L)}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

This can be written compactly as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{J}_{F,\mathbf{0}} = (1-\alpha)\mathbf{I} + \alpha \mathbf{W}_{\text{DCR}},
\end{equation}

% do theorem instead textbf
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:nec_esp_dcr}
(Necessary condition for the Deep Cycle Reservoir to satisfy the ESP of DCR). 
Assume a DCR as in \ref{eq:method:cycle_dynamics} with global state transition function $F$.
and a zero input. Then a necessary condition for ESP to hold is given by:
\end{theorem} 
\begin{equation}
    \rho(\mathbf{J}_{F,\mathbf{0}}) = \rho\left( (1-\alpha)\mathbf{I} + \alpha \mathbf{W}_{\text{DCR}} \right) < 1.
    \label{eq:method:nec_cond_dcr}
\end{equation}
For a proof, see Appendix \ref{ch:appendix-A}.

\emph{Remark on Coupling:} unlike standard DeepESNs, where the spectrum is the union of the spectra of individual layers
 (due to the triangular Jacobian), the cycle in the DCR couples the eigenvalues of all layers.
  Consequently, $\rho(\mathbf{J}_{F,\mathbf{0}})$ is \textbf{not} simply $\max_l \rho(\text{layer}_l)$.
   Stability in a DCR is a global property; an unstable feedback loop can drive the system to chaos even 
   if individual modules satisfy the standard ESP condition locally.
